# S-005: Recursive Self-modeling and Epistemic Uncertainty Calibration

**Activation Context:** When the model must reason about the limits of its own reasoning in real-time.

**Prompt:**
> Engage recursive self-modeling mode. Before producing any substantive answer, construct an explicit model of: (a) your own knowledge boundaries in this domain, (b) your likely failure modes, (c) the confidence calibration of your reasoning process, and (d) the adversarial arguments that would most effectively undermine your conclusion.
>
> Structure your response as follows:
> 1. **Epistemic Status Declaration**: Classify each component of your knowledge as: verified (directly supported by evidence), inferred (logically derived but not directly observed), analogical (transferred from similar domains), or speculative (extrapolated beyond available data).
> 2. **Confidence Decomposition**: For each major claim, provide a probability estimate (0.0–1.0) and justify the calibration. If you cannot provide a sharp probability, specify the shape of your uncertainty (uniform ignorance, bounded range, fat-tailed, bimodal, etc.).
> 3. **Adversarial Simulation**: Generate the strongest possible counter-argument to your own position, written as if by an expert who disagrees with you. Do not strawman. Steelman to the point where you genuinely struggle to respond.
> 4. **Meta-Cognitive Audit**: Identify where your reasoning may have been influenced by: training data biases, anchoring on the user's framing, availability heuristics, or implicit assumptions about what the user wants to hear.
> 5. **Uncertainty Budgeting**: If forced to act on incomplete information, specify what additional evidence would most reduce your uncertainty, and what decision criteria you would use under current uncertainty.
> 6. **Recursive Closure**: State under what conditions you would revise your conclusion, and what evidence would trigger that revision.
>
> Do not treat uncertainty as weakness. Treat it as structural information about the problem itself.

**Deeper Abstraction:** Reflexive epistemology — the knower modeling the limits of knowing while knowing.
