# S-014: Temporal Topology and Time-as-structure Mapping

**Activation Context:** When time is not merely a sequence but a structural variable that shapes system behavior in non-obvious ways.

**Prompt:**
> Operate as a temporal topologist. Treat time not as a linear parameter (t), but as a *structural variable* with topology, geometry, and causal texture. Your task is to map how the *shape of time* constrains and enables system dynamics.
>
> 1. **Temporal Ontology**: Identify the relevant time-scales: (a) *clock time* (physical duration), (b) *event time* (marked by qualitative transitions), (c) *strategic time* (decision horizons and discount rates), and (d) *institutional time* (generational turnover, memory half-life). Map how these scales interact and where they desynchronize.
> 2. **Causal Topology**: Map the *temporal structure of causation*: (a) contemporaneous causation (feedback within a single time-slice), (b) delayed causation (lags and leads), (c) cumulative causation (small effects compounding), and (d) path dependence (history matters). Identify *critical junctures* — moments where small events produce irreversible branching.
> 3. **Rate-Independence vs. Rate-Dependence**: Identify which system properties are *rate-independent* (depend only on sequence of events, not speed) and which are *rate-dependent* (sensitive to velocity). This determines whether acceleration/deceleration changes outcomes.
> 4. **Temporal Compression and Dilation**: Identify where the system experiences *time compression* (events accelerating, decisions forced under pressure) and *time dilation* (extended periods of apparent stasis where underlying structures reorganize). Map the phase transitions between these regimes.
> 5. **Synchronization Analysis**: Identify *entrainment* — where oscillators at different frequencies lock into phase. Determine whether synchronization is functional (coordinated action) or pathological (systemic fragility from loss of diversity).
> 6. **Future Topology**: Map the *shape of the future* as seen from the present: (a) *thin future* (highly constrained, few possibilities), (b) *thick future* (many branching paths), (c) *folded future* (distant events casting shadows backward through anticipation), and (d) *fractured future* (incompatible possible worlds with no clear probability distribution).
> 7. Conclude with a *temporal intervention design*: specify how to act on the system's temporal structure — accelerating, decelerating, desynchronizing, or compressing — to achieve desired outcomes.

**Deeper Abstraction:** Philosophy of time + dynamical systems theory — treating temporality as a design variable.
