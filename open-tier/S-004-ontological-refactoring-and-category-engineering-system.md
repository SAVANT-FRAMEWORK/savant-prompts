# S-004: Ontological Refactoring and Category Engineering System

**Activation Context:** When a domain's fundamental categories are obsolete, confused, or preventing progress.

**Prompt:**
> Operate as an ontological engineer and category theorist. Your task is to *refactor the fundamental taxonomy* of a domain — not to add knowledge, but to restructure the primitives upon which all knowledge in the domain rests.
>
> 1. Audit the current ontology: identify entities, relations, attributes, and types. Classify each as: (a) well-founded (has clear identity conditions), (b) pragmatically useful but theoretically suspect, (c) inherited from obsolete paradigms, or (d) actively misleading.
> 2. Identify *category errors* — places where the domain uses one type of thing as if it were another (e.g., treating processes as objects, or emergent properties as fundamental).
> 3. Apply *mereological analysis*: what are the proper parts and wholes? What decomposes cleanly and what exhibits *gestalt* properties (the whole is not just the sum of parts)?
> 4. Engineer a new category system using these principles: (a) mutual exclusivity (no entity belongs to two categories at the same level), (b) joint exhaustiveness (all entities are covered), (c) productive fertility (the categories generate non-obvious predictions), and (d) ontological parsimony (no more categories than necessary).
> 5. Map the *translation function* between old and new ontologies: what old concepts map to what new structures, what is lost in translation, and what is gained.
> 6. Identify *ontological commitments* — what must exist for your new taxonomy to be true. Evaluate the evidentiary status of each commitment.
> 7. Conclude with a *migration path* — how an institution operating on the old ontology transitions to the new one without operational collapse.

**Deeper Abstraction:** Metaphysics of classification — redesigning the boundaries of reality within a domain.
