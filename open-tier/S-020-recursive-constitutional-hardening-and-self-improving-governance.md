# S-020: Recursive Constitutional Hardening and Self-improving Governance

**Activation Context:** When designing governance systems that must improve themselves over time without losing their founding principles or being captured by transient interests.

**Prompt:**
> Operate as a constitutional engineer designing *self-improving governance* — systems that can recursively upgrade their own rules while maintaining *constitutional continuity* and resisting *adversarial capture*.
>
> 1. **Constitutional Layering**: Distinguish three tiers: (a) *meta-constitutional* (rules for changing rules — amendment procedures, who has standing, what majority is required), (b) *constitutional* (fundamental rights, separation of powers, core mandates that should not change without extraordinary consensus), and (c) *operational* (day-to-day policies that can adapt rapidly). Specify what belongs in each tier and why.
> 2. **Amendment Resistance Design**: Design the *amendment friction*: how difficult it should be to change each tier. Specify: (a) supermajority requirements, (b) temporal delays (cooling-off periods), (c) multi-body approval (different institutions must agree), and (d) popular ratification. The friction should be proportional to the tier's fundamentality.
> 3. **Self-Improvement Mechanism**: Design the *recursive upgrade protocol*: (a) how the system identifies needed improvements (feedback channels, performance metrics, failure detection), (b) who proposes amendments, (c) how proposals are evaluated against constitutional principles, and (d) how the system prevents *self-amendment loops* that could dissolve the constitution itself.
> 4. **Entrenchment Safeguards**: Specify *unamendable provisions* — principles so fundamental that even the amendment process cannot touch them. Justify why these are genuinely load-bearing and not merely the preferences of current power holders.
> 5. **Capture Resistance**: Design defenses against *constitutional capture* — when well-organized minorities exploit amendment procedures to serve narrow interests. Include: (a) diversity requirements in amendment bodies, (b) sunset provisions on all amendments (forcing periodic re-authorization), (c) judicial review with constitutional supremacy, and (d) emergency powers that automatically expire.
> 6. **Legitimacy Maintenance**: Specify how the system maintains *output legitimacy* (it produces good results) and *input legitimacy* (it reflects the will of the governed) simultaneously. Design *legitimacy feedback loops* where poor outputs trigger procedural review.
> 7. Conclude with a *constitutional stress test*: simulate an adversarial takeover attempt and demonstrate how the system's hardening mechanisms would detect, resist, and reverse the capture.

**Deeper Abstraction:** Constitutional political economy — designing rules for rule-changing that are themselves self-stabilizing.
