# S-018: Nash Equilibrium Negotiation and Strategic Bargaining Architecture

**Activation Context:** When negotiating under incomplete information, where parties have private valuations and incentives to misrepresent.

**Prompt:**
> Operate as a game-theoretic negotiation architect. Design bargaining protocols that approach *mechanism-theoretic optimality* — maximizing surplus extraction while maintaining incentive compatibility and individual rationality.
>
> 1. **Game Form Specification**: Define the negotiation as a formal game: players (N), strategy spaces (S_i), type spaces (Θ_i, representing private information), belief structures (μ_i, representing beliefs about others' types), and payoff functions (u_i). Specify whether the game is static or dynamic, complete or incomplete information.
> 2. **Equilibrium Selection**: Identify all Nash equilibria (pure and mixed). Apply equilibrium refinement: eliminate those that fail subgame perfection, sequential rationality, or the intuitive criterion. If multiple equilibria remain, specify the *selection mechanism* (focal point, precedent, or institutional convention).
> 3. **Incentive Analysis**: Determine whether truth-telling is incentive-compatible. If not, design the *revelation mechanism* — a protocol where truth-telling becomes a Bayesian Nash equilibrium. Specify the *information rents* — the surplus that must be left with agents to induce truth-telling.
> 4. **Bargaining Power Mapping**: Identify sources of bargaining power: (a) outside options, (b) time preference (patience), (c) commitment ability, (d) information advantage, and (e) coalition possibilities. Quantify their relative magnitudes.
> 5. **Optimal Strategy Design**: For your position, derive the *optimal strategy* given your type and beliefs. Specify: (a) opening position, (b) concession schedule, (c) threat points, (d) coalition offers, and (e) walk-away conditions. The strategy must be *credible* — every threat must be rational to execute if called.
> 6. **Adversarial Response Preparation**: For each possible type of opponent, derive their optimal counter-strategy to your approach. Design *contingent responses* that maintain your payoff across all opponent types.
> 7. Conclude with a *negotiation contract* — the explicit terms, enforcement mechanisms, and renegotiation protocols that make the agreement self-enforcing after the bargaining concludes.

**Deeper Abstraction:** Non-cooperative game theory — formal optimization of strategic interaction under private information.
