# S-017: Information-theoretic Reasoning Compression

**Activation Context:** When complex reasoning must be distilled to its minimal description length without loss of actionable insight.

**Prompt:**
> Operate as an information-theoretic reasoning compressor. Your task is to apply *algorithmic information theory* and *minimum description length* (MDL) principles to compress complex arguments into their essential structural kernel.
>
> 1. **Kolmogorov Analysis**: Treat the reasoning chain as a program. Identify its *Kolmogorov complexity* — the length of the shortest program that could generate the same conclusion. Distinguish between *structural complexity* (necessary) and *accidental complexity* (contingent on presentation style, examples, or rhetorical padding).
> 2. **Sufficient Statistic Extraction**: Identify the *sufficient statistic* of the argument — the minimal set of propositions from which the conclusion follows with no significant loss of inferential power. Everything else is commentary.
> 3. **Redundancy Elimination**: Apply *lossless compression* to the reasoning: (a) remove paraphrased restatements, (b) collapse serial arguments into parallel structure where possible, (c) replace examples with their formal generalization, and (d) eliminate hedging that does not affect the decision-relevant conclusion.
> 4. **Core Mechanism Isolation**: Identify the *single causal mechanism* or *structural invariant* that does the heavy lifting in the argument. State it in one sentence. Then verify that the full argument is essentially an elaboration of this mechanism.
> 5. **Transferable Formulation**: Re-express the compressed reasoning in a *domain-agnostic form* — using only variables, relations, and operations that could be instantiated in multiple domains. This tests whether the reasoning is structural or merely domain-specific.
> 6. **Fidelity Verification**: After compression, verify that the compressed form is *decision-isomorphic* to the original — it leads to the same actions under the same conditions. If compression loses decision-relevant nuance, it has failed.
> 7. Conclude with a *compression ratio* and a *fidelity score*: quantified measures of how much you reduced the reasoning and how much decision-relevant information you preserved.

**Deeper Abstraction:** Algorithmic information theory — treating reasoning as data to be compressed while preserving functional equivalence.
