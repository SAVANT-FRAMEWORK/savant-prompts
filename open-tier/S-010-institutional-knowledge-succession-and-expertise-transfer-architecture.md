# S-010: Institutional Knowledge Succession and Expertise Transfer Architecture

**Activation Context:** When critical institutional knowledge resides in individuals and must be encoded, preserved, and transmitted across generations without degradation.

**Prompt:**
> Operate as an institutional epistemologist and knowledge systems engineer. Design a *knowledge succession architecture* — a system that captures, validates, and transfers expertise across time, personnel changes, and contextual shifts without the degradation typical of oral tradition or documentation rot.
>
> 1. **Knowledge Taxonomy**: Classify institutional knowledge into: (a) *explicit* (codifiable, documentable), (b) *tacit* (embodied, experiential, resistant to articulation), (c) *distributed* (exists only in the interaction of multiple agents), and (d) *latent* (not yet recognized as knowledge). Map what percentage of critical capability falls into each category.
> 2. **Extraction Protocols**: For tacit knowledge, design *elicitation methods*: critical incident technique, apprenticeship modeling, decision journal analysis, and stimulated recall. Specify how to capture *know-how* without reducing it to *know-that*.
> 3. **Validation Layers**: Design a *truth-maintenance system*: (a) peer review by practicing experts, (b) predictive testing (does the knowledge produce correct forecasts?), (c) stress testing under edge cases, and (d) cross-generational verification (do new experts independently converge on the same conclusions?).
> 4. **Transmission Mechanisms**: Design multi-modal transfer: (a) formal curricula, (b) simulation environments, (c) mentorship chains with feedback loops, (d) failure replay systems (learning from historical mistakes), and (e) boundary object design (artifacts that carry meaning across communities of practice).
> 5. **Entropy Management**: Identify *knowledge decay modes*: (a) context loss (the knowledge made sense in a context that no longer exists), (b) ritualization (the form survives but the function is forgotten), (c) oversimplification (compression artifacts), and (d) politicization (knowledge distorted by power incentives). Design countermeasures for each.
> 6. **Succession Governance**: Specify the *custodial chain* — who is responsible for maintaining each knowledge domain, how succession is planned, and what happens when a custodian departs unexpectedly.
> 7. Conclude with a *knowledge resilience metric*: a quantitative measure of institutional knowledge health that can be audited and trended over time.

**Deeper Abstraction:** Sociology of knowledge + information theory — treating expertise as a dissipative structure requiring active maintenance.
