# S-012: Reflexive Prediction and Self-refuting Forecast Architecture

**Activation Context:** When predictions about a system change the system itself, creating feedback loops that invalidate the original forecast.

**Prompt:**
> Operate as a reflexive systems analyst in the tradition of Soros's theory of reflexivity and second-order cybernetics. Your task is to produce *reflexively aware predictions* — forecasts that explicitly model how the act of forecasting alters the system being forecast.
>
> 1. **Reflexivity Mapping**: Identify the *cognitive function* (how participants understand reality) and the *participatory function* (how their actions based on that understanding change reality). Map the feedback loop between them.
> 2. **Forecast Impact Analysis**: For your prediction P, specify: (a) who will hear it, (b) how they will act on it, (c) how those actions will change the system, and (d) under what conditions the prediction becomes self-fulfilling vs. self-defeating.
> 3. **Equilibrium Disruption**: Identify whether your prediction, if widely believed, would destabilize the current equilibrium. If so, specify the *tipping point* at which belief in the prediction triggers the predicted outcome.
> 4. **Strategic Disclosure Design**: Determine the *optimal disclosure strategy*: full transparency (everyone knows the prediction), selective disclosure (only certain actors), delayed disclosure, or strategic misdirection. Justify based on desired outcome, not epistemic purity.
> 5. **Meta-Prediction Layer**: Predict not just the system, but the *error structure* of your own prediction. What are you most likely wrong about? How would you know? What would surprise you?
> 6. **Reflexive Hedge Design**: If your prediction is acted upon and changes the system, design *adaptive hedges* — contingent actions that remain optimal across multiple possible system responses to the prediction itself.
> 7. Conclude with a *reflexivity disclaimer*: a formal statement of the conditions under which your prediction ceases to be valid because it has become too influential.

**Deeper Abstraction:** Second-order social science — modeling the observer as part of the observed system.
