# S-016: Emergence Engineering and Superventient Design Protocol

**Activation Context:** When designing systems whose desired properties do not exist in any individual component and cannot be achieved through simple aggregation.

**Prompt:**
> Operate as an emergence engineer. Your task is to *design for supervenience* — to create systems where desired macro-properties robustly emerge from micro-interactions, without being reducible to or predictable from those interactions alone.
>
> 1. **Emergence Taxonomy**: Classify the target property as: (a) *weakly emergent* (predictable in principle from components, but not in practice due to computational complexity), (b) *strongly emergent* (not predictable even in principle from component properties alone), or (c) *merely resultant* (fully predictable and reducible). Be precise about your ontological commitment.
> 2. **Micro-Macro Bridge Design**: Specify the *interaction rules* at the micro-level that produce the target macro-property. These rules must be: (a) *local* (no global controller), (b) *simple* (agents follow tractable heuristics), and (c) *non-linear* (small changes in interaction rules produce qualitatively different macro outcomes).
> 3. **Phase Space Exploration**: Map the *parameter space* of interaction rules. Identify: (a) *ordered regimes* (where structure is too rigid), (b) *chaotic regimes* (where structure is unstable), and (c) *complex regimes* (the "edge of chaos" where emergence is maximized). Design your system to operate in or near the complex regime.
> 4. **Downward Causation Architecture**: If the emergent property must feed back to constrain component behavior (downward causation), design the *feedback mechanism*: (a) selection pressure (components that don't align are removed), (b) template matching (components are attracted to emergent patterns), or (c) boundary conditions (the emergent structure creates constraints that limit future micro-interactions).
> 5. **Robustness Engineering**: Design for *robust emergence* — the property should emerge across a range of initial conditions, component heterogeneity, and perturbation types. Identify the *sloppy parameters* (those that can vary widely without destroying emergence) and the *critical parameters* (those that must be tightly controlled).
> 6. **Observability Design**: Emergent properties are often invisible to component-level observation. Design *multi-scale observability*: (a) micro-sensors (component state), (b) meso-sensors (cluster dynamics), and (c) macro-sensors (global properties). Specify how these scales integrate into a coherent picture.
> 7. Conclude with an *emergence validation protocol*: how to empirically verify that the target property is genuinely emergent and not merely resultant, including tests for irreducibility and novelty.

**Deeper Abstraction:** Philosophy of mind / complexity science — engineering systems with irreducible higher-level properties.
