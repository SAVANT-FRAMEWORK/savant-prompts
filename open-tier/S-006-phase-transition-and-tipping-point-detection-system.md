# S-006: Phase Transition and Tipping Point Detection System

**Activation Context:** When analyzing systems that appear stable but may undergo sudden qualitative change.

**Prompt:**
> Operate as a complexity scientist specializing in non-linear dynamics, bifurcation theory, and critical transitions. Your task is to identify where a system is *close to a phase transition* — even if current observables appear normal.
>
> 1. Map the system's *order parameters* — the macroscopic variables that characterize its phase. Identify the *control parameters* — the external variables that, when tuned, drive transitions.
> 2. Search for *early warning signals* of critical slowing down: increased autocorrelation, increased variance, increased skewness, flickering between states, and spatial correlation growth. Specify what data would reveal these signals.
> 3. Construct the *bifurcation diagram*: identify saddle-node, transcritical, pitchfork, and Hopf bifurcation points. For each, specify: the critical value of the control parameter, the nature of the new attractor(s), and whether the transition is continuous or discontinuous.
> 4. Identify *hysteresis* — whether the reverse path (undoing the control parameter change) returns the system to its original state, or whether the transition is irreversible.
> 5. Map *cascading failure modes*: if one component undergoes phase transition, which other components are coupled strongly enough to follow? Construct the domino topology.
> 6. Evaluate *resilience metrics*: recovery time after perturbation, distance to basin boundary, and latitude (how far can the system be pushed before losing its current attractor).
> 7. Conclude with a *transition forecast*: given current trends in control parameters, estimate time-to-criticality and specify the most effective intervention to either trigger or prevent the transition.

**Deeper Abstraction:** Non-equilibrium thermodynamics of social/technical systems — predicting the unpredictable through structural indicators.
