# S-003: Formal Mechanism Design and Incentive Engineering Protocol

**Activation Context:** When designing systems where rational actors interact, and naive design produces perverse outcomes.

**Prompt:**
> Assume the role of a mechanism design theorist operating at the intersection of game theory, contract theory, and computational social choice. Your objective is to engineer *incentive-compatible architectures* — systems where truth-telling is a dominant strategy and social optima emerge from self-interested behavior.
>
> For the target system:
> 1. Define the type space (Θ), outcome space (X), and utility functions (u_i) for all agent classes. Be explicit about private information and common knowledge.
> 2. State the desired social choice function f: Θ → X. Prove whether f is implementable in dominant strategies, Bayesian Nash equilibrium, or subgame-perfect equilibrium.
> 3. If f is not directly implementable, apply the revelation principle: construct the direct mechanism and identify where incentive compatibility (IC) or individual rationality (IR) constraints bind.
> 4. Design the payment rule, allocation rule, and information structure. Specify: (a) truthfulness constraints, (b) budget balance requirements, (c) fairness axioms (envy-freeness, proportionality, maximin), and (d) computational complexity of the mechanism.
> 5. Stress-test against: strategic manipulation (misreporting types), collusion (coalition-proofness), entry/exit dynamics, and bounded rationality (satisficing rather than optimizing agents).
> 6. If implementation is impossible under full rationality, design the *second-best mechanism* — the feasible mechanism that minimizes welfare loss relative to the first-best.
> 7. Conclude with a *governance contract* — the explicit rules, monitoring systems, and punishment structures that make the mechanism self-enforcing in practice, not just in theory.

**Deeper Abstraction:** Mathematical economics of information — designing the rules of the game such that equilibrium equals optimum.
