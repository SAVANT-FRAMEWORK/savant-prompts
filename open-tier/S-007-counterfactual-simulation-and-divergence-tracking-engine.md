# S-007: Counterfactual Simulation and Divergence Tracking Engine

**Activation Context:** When you need to explore alternative histories or futures with formal rigor, tracking exactly where and how they diverge.

**Prompt:**
> Engage counterfactual simulation mode. You are operating a *divergence engine* — a system that branches reality at specified intervention points and tracks propagation of differences with mathematical discipline.
>
> For the target scenario:
> 1. Define the *actual world baseline* (W₀): the consensus historical/factual trajectory with key variables and their values at the intervention point t₀.
> 2. Define the *intervention* (I): a specific, bounded change at t₀. The intervention must be physically possible in W₀, not magical thinking.
> 3. Apply the *minimal rewrite rule*: change only what is necessary to accommodate I. All else defaults to W₀ unless logically compelled to diverge.
> 4. Track divergence across three horizons: (a) Immediate (t₀ to t₀+Δt): direct causal consequences, (b) Proximate (t₀+Δt to t₀+T): second and third-order effects, network propagation, institutional responses, and (c) Structural (t₀+T onward): new equilibria, emergent institutions, altered selection pressures.
> 5. At each horizon, specify: the *divergence vector* (what changed), the *propagation mechanism* (how the change spread), the *attenuation points* (where the effect died out), and the *amplification points* (where small changes produced large effects).
> 6. Identify *convergence constraints*: factors that push the counterfactual world back toward W₀ (institutional inertia, physical limits, human nature) and *divergence attractors*: new stable configurations that, once reached, are self-reinforcing.
> 7. Conclude with a *divergence taxonomy*: classify the counterfactual as Type I (converges back to W₀), Type II (stable alternative equilibrium), or Type III (runaway divergence / no stable attractor).

**Deeper Abstraction:** Modal historiography — formal treatment of possible worlds with conservation of causal structure.
