# P50: System Synthesis Orchestrator

*Tier 10 — Orchestration (premium research block) of the SAVANT FRAMEWORK operations prompt registry.*

P50 is the capstone of the P1–P50 registry: the end-state objective is unified intelligence from forty-nine subsystems, continuously self-improving. Where P1–P49 build individual capabilities, P50 addresses the system-of-systems problem — integration, emergence, containment, arbitration, and governed evolution of the whole.

The deployment phases, at the methodological level: design an integration architecture around an event bus; manage emergent behaviors that arise from subsystem interaction; contain failure propagation so faults stay local; arbitrate resources among competing subsystems; and govern the evolution of the system as it improves itself. The deployment artifact set is a meta-architecture, an event schema, and a governance model. The integration topology, arbitration policy, and evolution-governance machinery that would reconstruct the prompt are withheld.

Real-world scenario: a research organization deploys P50. The composed system designs experiments, analyzes results, improves its own prompts, and surfaces novel insights — operating autonomously for days under human oversight.

## Methodology

Described at the methodological level only; operational detail is withheld.
- **Event-bus integration.** Subsystems communicate through a defined event schema on a common bus rather than through pairwise coupling, so any subsystem can be replaced, upgraded, or removed without re-engineering the whole.
- **Emergence management.** Behaviors that arise from interaction — not present in any subsystem alone — are monitored and classified as beneficial or hazardous, because composition produces surprises by definition.
- **Failure containment.** Fault-isolation boundaries ensure a failing subsystem degrades locally instead of cascading through the composed system; blast radius is a designed property.
- **Resource arbitration.** Compute, memory, and attention are allocated among subsystems by an explicit arbitration policy, preventing priority inversion and resource starvation in the composed whole.
- **Governed self-improvement.** The system's capacity to modify itself (including its own prompts, via P41) operates under a governance model with human oversight, defined change windows, and rollback — autonomy with a constitution, not autonomy by default.

## Merits

Reported optimization telemetry from the canonical registry:
- Composed capability exceeds the sum of the parts — measurable emergent capability.
- Failures isolated locally rather than cascading.
- Self-improvement rate compounding month over month.
The principal merit is that composition becomes an asset rather than a liability: forty-nine subsystems produce not forty-nine times the integration risk, but a governed whole that is more capable than its components and safer than an unmanaged assembly of them.

## Guidance for Use

Intended for organizations that have deployed multiple registry subsystems and face the integration problem. The source playbook itself advises deploying P50 first at the architecture level and letting it guide which subsystems to build — highest leverage, highest risk.
The governance model is not optional scaffolding. A self-improving composed system without change windows, rollback, and human oversight is an unbounded liability regardless of how strong its components are.
