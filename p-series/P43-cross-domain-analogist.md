# P43: Cross-Domain Analogist

*Tier 9 — Recursive (premium research block) of the SAVANT FRAMEWORK operations prompt registry.*

P43 systematizes analogical transfer: the end-state objective is novel solutions imported from unexpected domains, then validated and adapted to the target problem. It replaces accidental analogy ('this reminds me of...') with a structured search for isomorphic problem structures across fields.

The deployment steps, at the methodological level: define the target problem's structure; search other domains for problems isomorphic to it; map the structural correspondences; adapt the imported solution to the target domain; and test and iterate. The deployment artifact set is an analogy map, an adaptation plan, and validation results. The structural-representation scheme and the correspondence-mapping procedure that would reconstruct the prompt are withheld.

Real-world scenario: a logistics company uses P43, applying protein-folding algorithms to route optimization. Delivery efficiency improves 15% with the same compute budget.

## Methodology

Described at the methodological level only; operational detail is withheld.
- **Structural problem definition.** The target problem is described in terms of its abstract structure — constraints, objective, state space, dynamics — stripped of domain vocabulary, because analogy operates on structure, not on surface features.
- **Isomorphic search.** Other domains are searched for problems sharing that structure. Protein folding and vehicle routing, for instance, are both constrained combinatorial-optimization problems over large configuration spaces.
- **Correspondence mapping.** Elements of the source solution are mapped to elements of the target problem explicitly, so the adaptation is a designed transformation rather than a hopeful gesture.
- **Domain adaptation.** The imported method is modified for the target domain's constraints, scale, and data characteristics; an analogy supplies the idea, not the implementation.
- **Empirical validation.** Adapted solutions are tested against the incumbent approach, because the base rate of analogical transfer is low and most candidate analogies should fail validation.

## Merits

Reported optimization telemetry from the canonical registry:
- More than three novel candidate solutions generated per problem.
- Adaptation success rate above 50%.
- Breakthrough outcomes (order-of-magnitude improvement) in more than 10% of cases.
The principal merit is access to a solution inventory no single domain possesses: decades of another field's optimization effort become available for the price of a structural mapping and a validation run.

## Guidance for Use

Intended for research, operations-research, and advanced engineering teams facing problems that are hard within their own field's toolkit. Invest the effort in the structural problem definition; weak structure descriptions yield superficial analogies.
Hold the validation bar high. The methodology's value comes precisely from generating many candidates and discarding most of them — a team that falls in love with the first elegant analogy will underperform the process.
